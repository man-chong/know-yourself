# Phase 4 — Change one thing

Insight changes nothing on its own. This method is unusually good at producing the
*feeling* of progress — a person can run phases 1–3 repeatedly, learn something true
each time, and behave identically for years.

This phase is short on purpose. One behaviour, made countable, with a date.

## Pick one

Not three. Not a theme. **One behaviour, stated as something a person does or doesn't
do**, narrow enough that you could argue about whether it happened today.

Choose it from the **widest gap between a direction they named in phase 0 and what the
data shows they do.** That's what makes it theirs: the change moves them toward somewhere
they said they wanted to go, instead of toward an improvement you picked for them.

| too vague | usable |
|---|---|
| be more assertive | say "no" out loud when I decide against something |
| stop overthinking | when I want to send a fourth message, write it in a note instead |
| have better boundaries | stop work at a fixed hour on weekdays |
| be less anxious in dating | no messages between 1am and 6am |

The test: **could two people watching your week disagree about whether you did it?**
If yes, it's still too vague.

## Make it countable, and write down today's number

Go back to the data and get the current rate before anything changes. Without a
baseline there is nothing to compare to in phase 5, and memory is worthless here —
people reliably believe they've changed more than they have, and occasionally much
less.

```
direction   the people I love know where they stand with me
behaviour   saying no out loud instead of going quiet
metric      messages starting with no / don't / not now, as a share of replies
            to a suggestion
today       5 of 252 offers declined out loud  (2.0%)
different   any month where it's above 8%
date        1 December 2026
script      measure.py, unchanged
```

That block is the entire deliverable of this phase. It fits on an index card, and it
should.

## Choose the one with a real cost, not the comfortable one

The most useful finding from the only repeat measurement in this method's history:

> Two changes were written down in August. Three weeks later, one had gone from **0
> occurrences to 8** and the other **hadn't moved at all**.
>
> The one that moved was saying "you're right" — generous, free, no exposure.
> The one that didn't was stating a position that could be argued with.
>
> The person had unconsciously picked the zero-risk half of their own plan.

So when someone chooses their one thing, ask: **what does this cost you if you do it?**
If the answer is nothing, it will get done and it won't matter. The change worth
tracking is the one with something at stake.

This isn't an argument for picking the hardest thing. It's an argument for noticing
which half you reached for, because that choice is itself a measurement.

## Replace, don't remove

A rule that asks someone to *stop* doing something, with nothing in its place, breaks
under pressure. Not from weakness — because "do nothing" often isn't an available
action for the person in that moment. The urge is to discharge something, and removing
the outlet doesn't remove the urge.

Give it somewhere to go:

- instead of *don't send the fourth message* → **write it in a note, read it tomorrow**
- instead of *don't work past midnight* → **something specific that happens at 11pm**
- instead of *don't over-explain* → **say the one sentence, then stop and count to five**

Nine times out of ten the written message never gets sent. The tenth time it gets sent
better.

## Write it where it'll be found

A file in a repo, a note, wherever the person actually looks. It needs to survive
until the date, and phase 5 needs to be able to find the baseline number.

Two sentences of context help — what the pattern is and why this change — so that the
person in December understands what the person in September meant.

## Then stop

This is the end of the cycle, not a checkpoint before more analysis.

Say so explicitly. The instinct at this point — for the person and for you — is to
keep going: one more archive, one more cut, a deeper look at the thing that just came
up. That instinct is exactly what phase 5's date exists to interrupt.

Three months is the shortest interval that means anything. Six is better for anything
slower than a messaging habit.
