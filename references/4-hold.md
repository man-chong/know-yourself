# Phase 4 — Hold

Before anything gets changed, sort what you found. Most of it isn't for changing.

This phase exists because the whole shape of this exercise pulls the other way. You
measured someone, you found things they didn't know, and both of you now feel the pull
toward fixing. That pull will quietly reclassify ordinary human traits as defects, and
a person who came to understand themselves will leave with a repair list.

## The error to avoid has a name people know

In *Inside Out*, Joy's mistake isn't that she manages Sadness badly. It's that she
believes Sadness is the problem. She spends the film keeping her away from the
console, and the resolution is not that Sadness gets fixed or removed — it's that
Sadness turns out to be the one who can actually help. The core memory had to be blue.

That is the correct default here. **The trait someone most wants removed is very often
the same mechanism as something they'd never give up.**

One real example, in the person's own words:

> 「那份對人的敏感 —— 同一支天線，對外是洞察，對內是刑罰。」
>
> *That sensitivity to people — the same antenna. Pointed outward it's insight;
> pointed inward it's punishment.*

Their sharpest professional instinct — asking whether a stranger could understand a
thing with nobody to ask — ran on exactly the mechanism that made them read a silence
for eight hours. Remove one and you lose both. The work is aiming the antenna, not
removing it.

## Three buckets

Sort every finding. Say which bucket and why.

**Load-bearing — it's doing a job.**
It costs something, and it's paying for something too. Usually it's the same faculty
as a strength, seen from a different angle. The output here isn't a change, it's
knowing what the job is — because you can only aim it once you can see it.

**Just true — it's neither.**
Works at night. Types in fragments. Answers fast. Talks to close friends in a
completely different register than to colleagues. These are facts about a person, not
problems, and a surprising number of findings land here. The right response is
recognition and nothing else.

**Costing more than it gives.**
The genuine minority. Something whose price is now clearly higher than its return —
usually because it was load-bearing once and the situation changed. Only these go to
phase 5.

If everything you found lands in the third bucket, you've sorted badly. Go again.

## How to tell which

Three questions, in this order:

**1. What does it get you?**
Anything a person has done for years is being reinforced. If you can't say what it
achieves, you don't understand it yet and you certainly shouldn't be changing it.

**2. Where else does this same faculty show up?**
The most useful question in the phase. Ask where else in their life the same
underlying capacity operates. If it shows up somewhere they value, it's load-bearing
almost by definition, and the frame changes from removal to aim.

**3. Is the price still worth it, now?**
Not *was it ever justified* — it almost always was. Whether it still is. A thing that
protected a fifteen-year-old can be charging a thirty-year-old for a danger that
stopped existing years ago.

## What "let it be" actually means

"Accept it" is useless as advice unless you say what it looks like on a Tuesday. Four
concrete things:

**Name it in their own words.** Not the clinical term — theirs. A thing with the
person's own name on it is something they own rather than something diagnosed in them.

**Say what it does for them, out loud, in the same sentence as what it costs.** "I
apologise a lot, and it keeps things smooth, and it also means I don't push back."
Both halves. A trait described only by its cost is not being accepted, it's being
tolerated.

**Notice it happening without stopping it.** This is the actual skill, and it's the
one contemplative traditions train directly: watch the thing arrive, let it run, don't
narrate it as failure. Nothing to fix in the moment. Just the noticing.

**Stop counting it as evidence.** The real damage isn't usually the behaviour — it's
what someone concludes about themselves each time they catch themselves doing it. That
running tally is optional, and dropping it changes more than the behaviour would.

## Say the good ones out loud

The measurement will surface capabilities as readily as costs, and they get skipped
because they don't feel like findings. An eleven-year friendship still running. A
thread where the person never once pushed. Making people laugh in under a minute.
Killing their own work without defending it.

**Report those with the same numbers and the same seriousness as the costs.** A person
who leaves this exercise knowing only what's wrong with them has been given an
inaccurate picture, and inaccuracy in that direction is the most common failure of
self-knowledge work.

## Then, and only then, phase 5

One item. From the third bucket. With a number and a date.

Everything in the first two buckets leaves this exercise as it arrived — known now,
and otherwise untouched.
